Every reWord level hands you a jumbled sentence and a simple job: drag words until the sentence reads well. A few habits make that job faster and more fun. Here is a practical routine you can use from the very first level.

Start with the anchors

Some levels lock certain words in place. Locked words are your anchors, because they cannot move and every other word must fit around them. Read the locked words first, then ask where they sit in the sentence. A locked "big" next to a locked "slow" tells you an adjective pair is already built, and you only need to find the noun that follows and the verb that precedes.

Find the subject and the verb

Most sentences are built on a subject and a verb. Look for the word that names who or what is acting, then look for the action. In the level with "and", "turtle", "big", "slow", "the" and "was", the phrase "the turtle was" practically assembles itself once you spot the subject. After that, the leftover words fall into place as a description of the turtle.

Look for natural pairs

Many words travel together. Articles such as "the" lead into nouns. Words like "went to" sometimes arrive as one tile, so treat a multi word tile as a single building block. When you see two tiles that clearly belong side by side, move one next to the other and then treat them as one unit. This shrinks a six tile puzzle into a four tile puzzle.

Use capital letters and punctuation

The first word of a sentence often starts with a capital letter, and the last word often carries a period. In a level such as "The quick fox jumps over the lazy dog.", the capital "The" and the final "dog." point to both ends of the sentence immediately. Place those two first, and the middle becomes a smaller puzzle.

Swap with a plan

You swap a word by dragging it left or right to trade places with its neighbour. Because each swap moves two words, plan the order of your swaps. Work from one end of the sentence toward the other, fixing each position in turn. Once the first word is correct, you can keep your focus on the remaining tiles.

Watch the move counter

Some levels show a Moves counter instead of a clock, so a limited number of swaps is available. Before you drag, picture the finished sentence and count how many words need to travel. Choosing the swap that puts two words right at once is the most efficient move.

Use Hint as a coach

The Hint button highlights a word that is out of place. Treat it as a coach rather than a last resort. After a hint, ask yourself why that word belongs elsewhere. Was it a grammar clue, a capital letter, or a natural pair? Answering that question trains your eye for the next level.

Read the sentence aloud in your head

When you think you are done, read the sentence from start to finish. Your ear is a fast checker for word order. If a phrase sounds smooth, it is probably correct, and the level will confirm it by completing as soon as every word sits in the right spot.

A quick routine

  1. Read the locked words and note where they sit.
  2. Find the capital letter and the punctuation to mark the ends.
  3. Locate the subject and the verb.
  4. Join natural pairs into units.
  5. Swap from one end to the other.
  6. Press Hint when you want a nudge, then learn from it.

The best way to build these habits is to play. Each level is short, and your progress is saved so you can pick up where you left off.
